The invitation

We’re not looking for an ad. We’re looking for a Tuesday.

FORya plans what you’ll wear around the thing you’re actually going to — a dinner, an interview, a wedding in a field in October. It reads the date, the place and the weather, looks at what’s already in your wardrobe, and builds the outfit from there.

We call those Moments. Not events. Not occasions. The small, specific things that make getting dressed harder than it needs to be.

What we’d like from you is not a product demo. It’s one honest piece of content about a real Moment in your own week, and what it looked like to plan for it without the usual half hour of standing in front of a wardrobe.

If that sounds like something you’d post anyway — only easier — you’re exactly who this is for.

The invitation02

In three parts

What the app actually does. No more than that.

01

Moments

You tell it what you’re going to, where and when. It handles the dress code, the weather and the hour of day so you don’t have to think about any of it.

02

Your wardrobe

Photograph things you already own and they become part of the plan. The cut-out happens on your phone — the photo isn’t uploaded anywhere.

03

The look

A complete outfit, built from what you own plus anything missing. If a piece needs buying, it shows you where and what it costs. Nothing is hidden behind a limit.

Please don’t describe it as a shop, a styling service or an AI stylist that buys things for you. It plans; you decide; the retailer sells. That boundary matters to us and to the ASA.

Who we’re asking

Taste, not reach. We mean it.

We’re working with a deliberately small group. We’d rather have fifteen creators whose feeds we genuinely like than three hundred who post once. What we look at:

  • A point of view. Your feed looks like a person, not a catalogue.
  • You already get dressed on camera. Getting-ready content, outfit planning, wardrobe edits — it’s a format you’re fluent in.
  • Comments that read like conversation. We’d take 4,000 engaged over 90,000 quiet.
  • UK-based, because the catalogue, the pricing and the weather are UK right now.

What we don’t ask for

A follower minimum. A posting schedule that takes over your month. Scripted lines read to camera.

Exclusivity beyond the obvious: please don’t post for a directly competing styling app inside the campaign window.

And never a positive review. If you use it and it doesn’t work for you, tell us and we’ll pay you for your time anyway. That is a real offer.

Four ways in07

How to shoot it

Six things that make the difference.

  • Open on the problem. The first two seconds are the plan and the panic, never the app. “I’ve got a wedding in nine days and nothing that works.”
  • Show the real screens. Screen-record on your actual phone. Don’t recreate the interface, don’t speed it up so fast it can’t be read.
  • Natural light, real room. Your bedroom beats a studio. We are not trying to look expensive.
  • Say it once. One idea per video. Two features is one too many.
  • Keep the tone level. Confident and dry travels further than excited. No “OBSESSED”, no shouting, no three exclamation marks.
  • End on the outfit, not the logo. Wear the look. That’s the proof.

Captions: write them the way you write everything else. We will never send you a line to read. If a phrase doesn’t sound like you, cut it — including anything in this document.

How to shoot it08

The words

Say this. Not that.

Accurate

“It plans an outfit around what you’ve got on.”

“It uses clothes I already own.”

“It shows me what a missing piece costs and where to get it.”

“A Moment — a dinner, an interview, a wedding.”

“The cut-out happens on my phone.”

Not true, please avoid

“It buys the clothes for you.” — there’s no checkout in the app.

“It finds the lowest price.” — it shows sellers it can see, not the whole market.

“It knows your whole wardrobe.” — only what you’ve added.

“An event” or “an occasion.” — we say Moment, every time.

“AI stylist.” — it plans; you choose.

This isn’t pedantry. Claims we can’t evidence are the fastest way for both of us to end up in front of the ASA.

If you want to match us10

Non-negotiable

Label it as an ad. Clearly, and first.

  • #ad at the very start of the caption — not after a line break, not buried in a cluster of hashtags at the bottom, not “#sp” or “gifted”.
  • Switch on the platform’s paid partnership tool on both TikTok and Instagram, with FORya as the business partner.
  • Say it out loud or on screen in the first few seconds of the video too. The caption alone isn’t enough if the video travels without it.
  • Stories get it on every frame, not only the first.

Why we’re strict about it

UK advertising rules apply to paid creator content, and the responsibility sits with both of us. An unlabelled post can be removed and ruled against publicly.

It also just works better. Audiences have been able to spot an undisclosed ad for years, and the ones who notice stop trusting the rest of your feed, not just this post.

This clause is in the contract. We will ask for a correction before we ask for anything else.
